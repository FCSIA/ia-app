Flex Fridays
Flex Fridays at Innovation Academy are monthly STEM workshops aligned with each pathway. Every IA student must earn 12 Flex Friday credits per year to meet the magnet seal requirements. Each workshop equals 1 credit. Freshmen attend a dedicated Flex Friday each month, and each pathway holds a workshop about once a month. On Flex Fridays, students on campus collaborate on hands-on, project-based learning with peers and industry mentors. These real-world events are often sponsored by community partners, reinforcing IA’s STEM focus. Students off campus have a digital learning day: teachers provide assignments and independent learning activities to be completed at home. This asynchronous work helps develop student independence and ensure academic continuity even when not on campus.

Registration & Attendance: To earn credit, students must sign up in advance via the IA Events portal (event.fcsia.com) whenever they plan to attend a Flex Friday workshop. Failure to sign up or attend planned workshops will affect the earned credits. Flex Fridays are regular school days; if on campus, students attend the pathway workshops, and if not, they must complete assigned digital work at home. This system ensures everyone remains on pace academically while deepening their pathway learning.
FAQs:
Q: How many Flex Friday credits do I need? A: 12 per school year (earned through attending monthly workshops).
Q: What if I can’t attend a workshop on campus? A: You’ll complete assigned work at home (it’s still a school day).
Q: How do I sign up for a workshop? A: Log in to the IA Events portal (event.fcsia.com) and register before the Flex Friday.
Q: Who do I contact with questions? A: Mrs. Taylor (Flex Friday coordinator, TaylorLS@fultonschools.org) handles scheduling and questions.

Academics & Pathways
Innovation Academy is a STEM-focused magnet high school. All freshmen take a two-period “GT Block” introductory class that surveys all three STEM pathways: Health Science, Engineering, and Information Technology.

In GT Block, students earn credit in each pathway’s fundamentals. After GT Block, students select one major pathway in which to focus; many students also major and minor in an additional pathway because of their strong cross-disciplinary curriculum. Completing a pathway involves taking the full sequence of pathway-specific Career & Technical (CTAE) courses, culminating in pathway capstones. Each pathway has specialized tracks and resources:
Health Science Pathway: IA’s health sciences pathway offers tracks such as Biotechnology, Patient Care, Surgical Technology, Pharmacy, Emergency Medical Responder (EMR), and Allied Health. Students train in state-of-the-art simulation labs – including mock surgical suites, a faux ambulance, and advanced diagnostic equipment (e.g. patient simulators and phlebotomy mannequins). For example, one IA report notes that students “take charge” during Flex Friday “Save-a-Life” events, simulating medical emergencies to apply clinical skills. Health pathway students can earn industry-recognized certifications (e.g. CPR, phlebotomy) and may participate in HOSA competitions, reflecting IA’s healthcare focus.
Engineering Pathway: The engineering pathway covers fields from materials, biomedical, electrical, mechanical, mechatronics, aerospace, civil, to structural engineering, plus rigorous training in the engineering design process. Students can earn certifications like SolidWorks CAD. IA’s engineering wing is equipped with multiple labs: 3D printers of various types, CNC milling and routing machines, laser cutters, welders (including virtual welding simulators), and even an aerospace wind tunnel. Robotics and manufacturing labs allow students to design and prototype real projects. The engineering curriculum is project-based, so students frequently work on open-ended challenges (e.g. rocketry, structural design, and biotech devices). These facilities give IA engineers “real-world” lab experiences beyond a typical high school.
Information Technology Pathway: The IT pathway includes Computer Science, Game Design, Cybersecurity, Embedded Computing, and starting in the 2025–26 year, an Artificial Intelligence track. IA’s computer labs are high-end: one room has gaming-grade machines and consoles for game-design classes, another houses a rack of servers for cybersecurity exercises. The AI track enables students to explore machine learning tools and projects. IA partners with tech industry and even hosts hackathons and coding clubs, aligning with its emphasis on cutting-edge IT skills.

Pathway Clubs and Collaboration: Each pathway has a corresponding student club where students further explore their fields outside class. On Flex Fridays and during other events, students from the same pathway work together on complex problems, integrating knowledge across their track (for example, engineering students tackling a civil-design challenge together).
FAQs:
Q: What is GT Block? A: A two-period freshmen course covering basics of all three pathways; students earn credits in Health Sci, Engineering, and IT, then choose a major pathway.
Q: Can I study more than one pathway? A: Yes – many students double-major or minor. After freshman year, you pick a primary pathway but can also take courses in others.
Q: What lab facilities are there? A: IA has advanced labs (surgical suites, ambulance, CNC/3D-printing shops, VR simulators, cybersecurity servers, etc.) corresponding to each pathway.
Q: What certifications or competitions? A: Students can pursue industry certifications (e.g. in health tech) and compete in related contests (HOSA, TSA, CyberPatriots). IA’s clubs and classes prepare them for these events.

Projects & Research
Innovation Academy emphasizes authentic research and capstone projects. All 11th graders take a required Scientific Research course, in which they complete the Pinnacle Project. The Pinnacle Project is a yearlong independent research or design project on a topic of their choice. Through the Pinnacle, students learn scientific methodology: proposing questions, conducting experiments or analyses, and presenting findings, often in competitions or fairs. IA also offers the AP Capstone Diploma program. Typically, 10th graders take AP Seminar and 11th graders take AP Research. Students who score 3 or higher on both AP Seminar and AP Research and four other AP exams earn the AP Capstone distinction. Many IA students pursue Capstone because of the school’s strong emphasis on research; in fact, the Course Catalog lists both AP Seminar and AP Research as part of its curriculum.
FAQs:
Q: What is the Pinnacle Project? A: A required junior year capstone research project in the Scientific Research course. Each student conducts an independent investigation under a teacher mentor, culminating in a formal report and presentation.
Q: What is AP Capstone? A: An optional diploma program: 10th graders take AP Seminar; 11th take AP Research. Scoring 3+ on Seminar, Research, and four other AP exams earns the AP Capstone diploma (a college system-recognized honor).
Q: When do these courses occur? A: AP Seminar is usually offered in 10th grade (as an honors English), and AP Research/Scientific Research is in 11th grade.
Q: Are other advanced research opportunities available? A: Yes, IA frequently hosts science fairs, local grant programs, and guest-lecturer seminars. Students can also participate in region/state science competitions beyond class.

Course Offerings & Credit Requirements
IA students complete all Fulton County graduation requirements plus the IA magnet requirements. Fulton requires 23 credits: 4 English, 4 Math, 4 Science, 3 Social Studies, 1 Health/Personal Fitness, 3 CTAE/World Language/Fine Arts, and 4 Electives. IA students fulfill these and add the magnet requirements: the freshman GT Block, a full STEM pathway sequence, the Pinnacle Project, and 12 Flex Friday credits per year. IA offers an extensive array of courses. Besides required core classes, students choose from many AP and honors options and CTAE courses. Advanced courses include AP Biology/Chemistry/Physics, Calculus AB/BC, AP Statistics, AP Computer Science A/Principles, AP Environmental Science, AP English Lang/Lit, AP History/Government/Economics, AP Psychology, AP Seminar/Research, and many more. The school profile lists over 20 AP offerings plus dual-credit options. Students may also dual-enroll part-time or full-time at local colleges (grades 10-12), earning high school and college credit concurrently.

Grade Scale: Fulton uses A=90–100, B=80–89, C=70–79, F<70. Honors/AP/Dual classes are weighted: 7 extra quality points are added to passing grades in these courses.
FAQs:
Q: How many credits are required to graduate? A: 23 total: 4 Eng, 4 Math, 4 Sci, 3 SS, etc. (Fulton reqs), plus IA magnet requirements (freshman GT, pathway courses, Pinnacle, Flex Fridays).
Q: What AP classes can I take? A: A wide range: biology, chemistry, physics (1/2/C), calculus AB/BC, stats, CS A/Principles, English Lang/Lit, econ, government, history, psychology, and more.
Q: Are there dual enrollment options? A: Yes – e.g., Georgia Tech math/CS classes and college Anatomy/Physiology courses. Dual enrollment is available for qualified 10th–12th graders.
Q: Are AP/Dual courses weighted? A: Yes, Fulton adds 7 quality points to passing grades in AP, honors, and dual-enrollment courses.

Daily Schedule & Special Schedules
IA has a flexible schedule model:
Regular Day (Mon–Thu): Eight periods; 1st begins at 8:55 AM and 8th ends at 4:15 PM. One period is lunch (students eat during 4th or 5th period). Ninth- and tenth-graders typically have one virtual/self-guided period.

Club Weeks: Several weeks each semester replace the normal schedule with a special club schedule. All 8 periods meet, but with a “flex” club period inserted between 4th and 5th periods each day (typically Tues–Thurs). Mondays in club weeks are set aside for other activities. IA holds club weeks each month for dedicated club time for students to immerse themselves in 1-3 clubs they are interested in during the school day.

Flex Fridays: Fridays follow the Flex Friday schedule. On many Fridays, freshmen have a designated workshop; on others, a particular pathway meets, or it’s a full “all student” day. Flex Fridays begin at the normal time but end earlier, after a 4-period schedule.
If IA has a teacher in-service (PD) on a Friday, school is not in session.
Example Bell Schedule: 1st 8:55–9:45, 2nd 9:49–10:39, 3rd 10:43–11:33, 4th 11:37–12:33, 5th 12:37–1:33, 6th 1:37–2:27, 7th 2:31–3:21, 8th 3:25–4:15.
FAQs:
Q: How long is the school day? A: Mon–Thu 8:55 AM–4:15 PM (8 periods). Fridays are shorter (Flex Friday schedule).
Q: What is “Club Week”? A: Certain weeks have all 8 classes plus a “flex” club period (between 4th & 5th) for club meetings.
Q: What if I miss a block day? A: Block schedules count as regular school days; absences are handled like any other day (see Attendance).
Q: When is lunch? A: Lunch is during 4th or 5th period (students choose either 11:37–12:33 or 12:37–1:33).

Attendance & Absences
IA students must maintain good attendance: at least 90% per year (no more than 10 total absences or tardies/early checkouts). Falling below 90% triggers an administrative review, which can result in probation or removal from the magnet program. IA allows an equal number of school days to make up work for any excused or unexcused absence. Teachers post missing assignments in Canvas with a zero (“Missing”) grade; once the student submits the work, the zero is replaced and a “Late” flag is added (with a 10‑point late penalty). Students should always check each teacher’s syllabus for any additional late-work guidelines.

Reporting Absences: Parents/guardians must notify the Attendance Office of all absences promptly (via the attendance line or email). After an absence, students get the same number of days as they were out to complete make-up work.

Pre-Approved Absences: For planned events (college visits, important family events, religious observances), students should submit a Pre-Approved Absence Form at least 5 school days in advance. Note: Family vacations are generally not approved. The principal reviews the student’s attendance and grades before approving. A pre-approved absence still requires make-up work on return.

Disciplinary Attendance: Chronic absenteeism or tardiness may lead to ISS or other discipline per Fulton policy. Students must sign in if arriving late: anyone coming after 2nd period must check in at the front office.
FAQs:
Q: What is the attendance policy? A: 90% attendance minimum (≤10 absences/tardies per year). Below that triggers a review and possible magnet probation.
Q: How do I make up work? A: You have as many days as you were absent to complete assignments for full credit. Missing work is marked “Missing” (zero) in Canvas until submitted. Late submissions incur a 10% penalty.
Q: What counts as tardy? A: Arriving late to school or leaving early without a valid excuse. Exceeding 10 total tardies/early checkouts is not allowed.
Q: How to get an absence excused? A: Provide documentation (e.g., doctor’s note) to the Attendance Office. Use the Pre-Approved form for planned absences (college visit, etc.).
Q: What if I have a meeting/appointment? A: You still need to notify Attendance and make up work. Try to schedule outside school hours if possible.

Morning Arrival & ID Lanyards
Students entering the building must wear their IA ID lanyard visibly at all times. Ninth graders wear solid lanyards (provided by IA); 10th–12th graders use color-coded lanyards by pathway (Green = Health Sci, Blue = IT, Yellow = Engineering). If a student forgets or loses their lanyard, they must report immediately to the front desk or the “eXchange” for a temporary pass, which must be returned when replaced. Repeated failure to display the lanyard is subject to disciplinary action.

Arrival Procedure: Students arriving by bus enter through the back entrance, and those by car enter the front. All students must have their lanyards visible to campus security. A student arriving late (after 2nd period) must sign in at the front office, regardless of lanyard status. (If arriving during the 1st period without a lanyard, they still show ID to security at the door or check in up front.) No one should buzz the back doorbell.

Cafeteria Note: During 4th and 5th periods (lunch), students are not allowed to leave campus (no going to cars, etc.) unless they are a senior with an off-campus lunch contract.
FAQs:
Q: What if I forget my lanyard? A: Go to the front desk or eXchange to get a temporary lanyard/pass. You must return it. Repeated infractions lead to consequences.
Q: Can I wear a personal lanyard? A: 9th graders must use a solid red lanyard (no other colors); 10th-12th graders must use their pathway color (or the replacement blue if lost).
Q: Where do I sign in if late? A: If you arrive after the 2nd period, sign in at the front office. If you arrive during the 1st/2nd, you may show your lanyard at the back door.
Q: Why are lanyards required? A: For school security and identification. It’s a Fulton policy to have student IDs visible at all times.

Parking & Transportation
Student Parking: Licensed IA students (16+) may park on campus with a parking permit. IA opens parking registration each semester; to apply, students must upload a copy of their driver’s license, vehicle registration, and insurance to the secure portal. The permit fee for Spring 2026 is $50. Note: Learner’s permit holders are not eligible for parking on campus. Permits are limited, so apply early.

Buses: Fulton County Schools provides bus transportation from each student’s home to their zoned high school to IA in the morning, and back to the home school in the afternoon. (For example, Milton HS students take the Milton → IA bus in the morning, and the IA → Milton bus home.) Bus schedules by zone are posted on the Fulton Schools Transportation site. Families are responsible for getting students to their home-school bus stop each day. Parents may also drop off/pick up at IA if preferred.

Sports Travel: IA fields a GHSA eSports team (Splatoon, Rocket League, etc.). Students wanting to play traditional sports (football, basketball, etc.) do so with their zoned high school teams. Fulton policy allows transfers in this case: students must submit a GHSA Transfer Eligibility Form through their home school. Many IA athletes take the 8th period virtually or off-campus so they can leave for practices. Speak with IA counseling for details on scheduling virtual periods for sports.
FAQs:
Q: How do I get a parking permit? A: Complete the online registration (upload license, insurance, reg) and pay the $50 fee. Permits are issued each semester.
Q: Can I park if I have a learner’s permit? A: No – only full driver’s licenses are eligible.
Q: Do IA students ride special buses? A: Yes – Fulton provides buses from your home high school to IA and back. Check the IA Transportation page for routes.
Q: Can I play sports at my home school? A: Yes – IA’s only school team is eSports. For other sports, see the home school’s coach and file a GHSA transfer form (IA guidance can assist).

Q: How do I start a club? A: Bring an idea to Student Life or the SGC; students with a common interest can petition to start new clubs under faculty sponsorship.

Support Resources
Counseling & Academic Support
IA has a guidance counselor team that assists with course planning, scheduling, and post-secondary preparation. Counselors meet with students each spring for the next year’s course selection (see the Grade-Level Scheduling guides online). They also help with career exploration and SAT/ACT prep strategies. Detailed scheduling info and graduation planning tools are provided on the Counseling & Academics site. Students in the Talented & Gifted (TAG) program receive additional support through IA’s GT coordinator.

College & Career Center
The College & Career Center provides year-round support for college applications and career planning. It offers college visits (many universities send reps to IA), career exploration events, SAT/ACT/NCAA eligibility workshops, and financial aid nights. The Center maintains announcements on Canvas and social media about upcoming events, scholarship deadlines, etc.. Staff help students write essays and navigate college applications. IA also offers dual-enrollment advising (Georgia Futures, etc.) in the center.
FAQs:
Q: Who helps with college planning? A: Talk to the IA counselors or visit the College & Career Center. They offer workshops on applications, financial aid, and hold college rep visits.
Q: Where do IA grads go? A: Graduates attend a wide range of colleges – many go into STEM fields (Georgia Tech, UGA, etc.) and selective universities. IA’s website has a list of recent colleges.
Q: Is there NCAA support? A: Yes – the counselors guide prospective D1/D2 athletes on NCAA eligibility.

Media Center & Technology
The school Media Center (library) is known as the eXchange. It supports student research and is a designated place where students can come together and study together. The exChange is two floors. The top floor is an individual silent student space, while the first floor is for group collaboration. The eXchange includes a STEM makerspace with tools for design prototyping, a podcast room which students can book if they need to record anything, and it has Baypods which are mini breakout rooms in the exchange where students can work if they need a quieter space to work. District portals (Infinite Campus for grades, Canvas for courses, FCS Portal for communications) are used. IA is a 1:1 school: every student has a district-issued laptop/tablet. District policies on digital citizenship apply.
Q: What is the eXchange? A: The eXchange is IA’s two floor media center where students can go to get work done in a controlled environment.
Q: What features are in the eXchange? A: The eXchange has baybods, a makerspace, and a podcast room.

Health Office
A school nurse is on campus daily. All Fulton health policies apply: immunization forms required at registration, medications logged through nurse’s office, and wellness visits managed by FCS guidelines.
Volunteering
Parents and family members wishing to volunteer (in class, chaperone field trips, etc.) must complete Fulton’s volunteer registration. This involves an online background check through the district’s volunteer portal. Once approved, volunteers can assist with clubs, events, or school activities.
