## Design Context

### Users

Innovation Academy students, teachers, club sponsors, and school administrators use the app throughout the school day to check schedules, coordinate clubs, read announcements, manage school operations, and communicate quickly from phones and computers.

### Brand Personality

Clear, capable, and dependable. The product should feel school-centered and organized without becoming institutional, childish, or sterile. Copy is concise and direct; the interface earns trust by making common actions obvious and fast.

### Aesthetic Direction

Refined native utility with readable density. Mobile should feel deliberately designed for touch and platform conventions while remaining visually consistent with the web app's Fluid Functionalism, shared tokens, and semantic states. Support every project theme. Favor clear hierarchy, strong typography, restrained color, whitespace, and progressive disclosure. Use rounded groups, subtle separators, and layered surfaces when they clarify relationships or explain a bounded content width. Avoid redundant frames, hard outlines, oversized pills, decorative gradients, repeated labels, and generic dashboard chrome.

The interaction quality bar is flagship rather than MVP: immediate feedback, stable layouts, responsive chat, useful long-press actions, 44-point targets, and thoughtful keyboard and safe-area behavior. Discord is a reference for conversational speed and progressive message actions, not for visual imitation.

### Design Principles

1. Make every word, icon, border, and container earn its place.
2. Keep the primary job visually dominant; disclose secondary controls through context and interaction.
3. Use shared semantic tokens and patterns so web and mobile feel related without forcing web UI onto native screens.
4. Prefer native-feeling, immediate interactions with clear pressed, loading, disabled, unread, and error states.
5. Design for real school-day conditions: one-handed use, long names and messages, dark and light themes, accessibility, and unreliable networks.

### Interaction and restraint

- Trust the reader. Prefer clear labels, recognizable controls, and immediate
  feedback to subtitles that explain an already obvious action. Keep help when
  it resolves real ambiguity, consequences, dependencies, or recovery. Do not
  repeat the selected navigation label as a panel title without adding context.
- Let affordances describe the actual target: pointer, hover, pressed, and focus
  states belong to the control that responds. Keep adjacent actions independent;
  avoid a clickable parent around a switch or another competing action.
- Make structure legible through alignment, spacing, and progressive disclosure.
  Keep icons and primary labels on one axis; place compact metadata alongside
  them when space permits. Use hierarchy cues for actual relationships.
- Space the content that exists. Optional controls must not leave phantom slots.
  Use consistent container edges to align actions. A frame or layered surface
  earns its place when it groups related controls or explains where content ends;
  remove it when it adds no useful structure. Keep useful density and quiet
  separators rather than flattening every group or spreading rows unnecessarily.
  Give each edge one owner; do not stack a border on an outline shadow or add
  elevation to a static group just to draw its boundary.
- Share the stronger implementation across routes that perform the same job.
  Choose primitives by what activation does, and repair geometry at its owning
  component rather than compensating in each page. Keep layout anchors stable
  when panels change height; reserve scrollbar space at the scroll owner.
